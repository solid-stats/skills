# Research: Architecture for a scheduled ingest/ETL-extract pipeline service in TypeScript/Node (a CLI run-once job: discover new replay files from an external HTTP source → fetch bytes → store raw objects in S3-compatible storage → write staging/outbox records to PostgreSQL, with checkpoint/resume; it must NOT parse file contents and must NOT touch the downstream system's business tables). What is actually known and practiced (2023–2026): (1) dominant architecture patterns for such pipelines in TS/Node — ports-and-adapters/hexagonal vs explicit pipeline-stage taxonomy vs onion/layered — and which best models "discover → fetch → persist with checkpoint/resume"; (2) how mature projects encode read-source / write-sink isolation as a structural rule (module boundaries, import-lint rules) that keeps an extractor from reaching into the system-of-record; (3) where checkpoint/resume and idempotency logic should live — an orchestration concern or a first-class layer; (4) how resilience primitives (retry/backoff/rate-limit/concurrency caps) are layered — cross-cutting utility vs adapter-decorator band; (5) published dependency-cruiser or eslint-boundaries rulesets for enforcing layer boundaries in Node services. Prefer evidence from real OSS ingest/ETL codebases (Airbyte connectors, Meltano/Singer taps, n8n, Temporal TS samples, AWS data pipelines) and recognized architecture literature.

## Summary
For a TS/Node scheduled ingest pipeline (discover → fetch → store raw to S3 → write outbox to Postgres with checkpoint/resume), hexagonal/ports-and-adapters is the most defensible structural choice: it directly models the "batch script driving external adapters" pattern from Cockburn's original definition, and its port/adapter split is the structural rule that prevents an extractor from reaching downstream business tables. However, for a pipeline with low domain complexity and no business rules, this architecture may be over-engineering — vertical slicing or a plain pipeline-stage taxonomy can work better. Resilience primitives (retry/backoff with exponential + jitter) are well-established as orchestrator-level concerns in OSS ingest tools, not per-adapter. Checkpoint/resume is an orchestration concern but is often immature or absent in OSS tools. Static boundary enforcement via dependency-cruiser or eslint-plugin-boundaries is available but the specific rulesets for ingest/ETL layer isolation could not be verified with primary evidence.

## Findings

### 🟢 Hexagonal architecture directly models the 'batch script driving application without touching runtime devices and databases' pattern. Its core value for an ingest pipeline is that ports (interfaces) define what the pipeline needs from outside (HTTP source, S3 sink, Postgres outbox), and adapters implement those ports — structurally preventing ingest logic from importing or calling downstream business tables. This is the canonical mechanism for read-source/write-sink isolation.
_Confidence: high_

Cockburn's original definition explicitly names 'batch scripts' as a driver alongside users and automated tests, and emphasizes 'developed and tested in isolation from its eventual run-time devices and databases.' The port/adapter split is the structural rule: ingest logic depends only on port interfaces, never on concrete adapter implementations or downstream table schemas.

Sources: <https://www.arhohuttunen.com/hexagonal-architecture/> · <Alistair Cockburn, 2005 — original hexagonal architecture definition>

### 🟢 Hexagonal/onion/concentric architectures are NOT appropriate when domain complexity is low (CRUD-like) or when the application challenge is not in business rule complexity. For a pipeline that only discovers, fetches, and stores raw bytes — no business rules — vertical slicing, an anemic model, or a plain pipeline-stage taxonomy (discover → fetch → persist stages as explicit modules) may be a better fit with less boilerplate.
_Confidence: high_

Victor Rentea (Jan 2023): 'If the domain complexity of your problem is fairly low (CRUD-like), or if the challenge of your application is NOT in the complexity of its business rules, then the Onion/Hexagonal/Ports-Adapters/Clean Architecture might not be the best choice, and you might be better off with vertical slicing, anemic model, CQRS, or another type of architecture.' A raw-bytes ingest pipeline with no parsing and no business logic sits exactly in this category.

Sources: <https://victorrentea.ro/blog/overengineering-in-onion-hexagonal-architectures/>

### 🟢 Resilience primitives — specifically exponential backoff with jitter — belong at the orchestration layer, not per-adapter. The verified pattern from OpenETL is: min(baseDelay × 2^attempt, maxDelay) + random(0–30% of delay), configured once in the pipeline orchestrator's error_handling settings and applied uniformly across all adapter calls.
_Confidence: high_

Direct implementation code from openetl confirms the formula and the orchestrator-level configuration. Placing retry/backoff in the orchestrator (not in each adapter) is consistent with the cross-cutting concern pattern and avoids duplicating retry semantics in every HTTP or S3 adapter.

Sources: <https://github.com/jspreadsheet/openetl — src/index.ts lines 67–77>

### 🔴 Checkpoint/resume and idempotency should be treated as first-class orchestration concerns — tracked state in a durable store (e.g., Postgres outbox/staging table) that the orchestrator reads before each run to determine what has already been processed. Checkpointing enables resumption but does NOT guarantee zero duplication on its own; at-least-once delivery with idempotency keys on the write side is required to suppress duplicates.
_Confidence: low_

The specific claim that checkpointing enables 'precise resumption without data loss or duplication' was refuted — 'without duplication' is false as a general statement. OpenETL's checkpoint/resume was listed as a low-priority future feature (not yet implemented) per the research, further indicating this is an immature area in available OSS TS/Node ingest tooling. The defensible position: track per-file/per-object processing state in Postgres (outbox pattern), use a unique key per discovered file, and rely on INSERT ... ON CONFLICT DO NOTHING or equivalent idempotency at the Postgres write step.

Sources: <General distributed systems literature — no primary OSS ingest codebase confirmed this pattern with verified code>

### 🔴 Static boundary enforcement tools (dependency-cruiser, eslint-plugin-boundaries) exist and can express import rules that prevent ingest-layer modules from importing business-table adapters. However, specific published rulesets for TS/Node ingest or ETL layer isolation were not verified with primary evidence from real OSS codebases during this research.
_Confidence: low_

Both tools support path-based from/to forbidden rules. Multiple specific entailment claims about their rulesets were refuted in adjudication (the supporting quotes did not entail the claims). What is defensible: dependency-cruiser's .dependency-cruiser.js forbidden rules and eslint-plugin-boundaries element/rule configuration can both express 'ingest modules must not import from system-of-record modules,' but no verified real-world ruleset example for this specific pattern was confirmed.

Sources: <https://github.com/sverweij/dependency-cruiser> · <https://github.com/javierbrea/eslint-plugin-boundaries>

## Caveats
1. Three confirmed claims and eleven refutations means most of the detailed architecture guidance (specific layer enforcement rules, boilerplate cost numbers, checkpoint implementation patterns) rests on unverified or weak evidence. The confirmed claims cover first-principles (Cockburn's definition, Rentea's CRUD caveat, OpenETL's backoff formula) but not practical implementation specifics for TS/Node ingest pipelines.

2. Checkpoint/resume implementation details are the weakest area: no OSS TS/Node ingest codebase was found with a verified, production-grade checkpoint/resume implementation. OpenETL explicitly has it as a future feature. Singer/Meltano tap patterns exist in Python but were not verified for TS.

3. The eslint-plugin-boundaries and dependency-cruiser findings are based on tool existence and general capability, not on verified published rulesets for ingest/ETL boundary enforcement. The specific behavioral claims about these tools (sequential rule evaluation, override semantics) were refuted.

4. The OpenETL source is labeled 'secondary' in the research but the adjudicator confirmed it is primary (direct code). The backoff formula is reliable.

5. Airbyte connector architecture (prominent OSS ingest reference) was not represented in any confirmed claim — its TS/Java connector patterns were not verified during research.

## Open questions
- What checkpoint/resume pattern do mature TS/Node ingest tools (e.g., Airbyte JS/TS connectors, Fivetran SDK) actually implement in code — outbox table, cursor column, or separate state store?
- Are there published dependency-cruiser or eslint-plugin-boundaries configurations in real OSS TS ETL/ingest projects that enforce source-adapter vs sink-adapter isolation as a lint rule?
- For a zero-domain-logic ingest pipeline, does a plain pipeline-stage module taxonomy (discover/fetch/persist as separate TS modules with explicit imports) outperform hexagonal in practice — lower onboarding cost, fewer abstractions — given that the confirmed literature says hexagonal is overkill for CRUD-level complexity?
- How do Temporal TS workflow samples handle idempotency for file-ingest activities — activity-level idempotency keys, saga compensation, or outbox pattern — and does that differ from a cron-job approach?

- ~~Checkpointing creates consistent recovery points by persisting processing state, enabling precise resumption without data loss or duplication.~~ — The claim states checkpointing enables "precise resumption without data loss or duplication." The "without duplication" portion is materially false as a general statement. Airbyte (the cited source) explicitly documents at-least-once delivery semantics, where the last records may be sent more than once "by design." Checkpointing prevents data loss but inherently permits duplication in standard implementations. Preventing duplication requires additional architectural mechanisms (idempotent sinks, upsert semantics, two-phase commit) beyond checkpointing alone. The claim is contradicted by the cited source's own documentation and by Confluent, Conduktor, Databricks, and Spark literature. The overstated "no duplication" guarantee is not a minor qualification — it reflects a fundamental mischaracterization of checkpointing semantics that could cause real architectural harm if acted upon. (<https://airbyte.com/data-engineering-resources/idempotency-in-data-pipelines>)
- ~~Modern data lakes and warehouses provide ACID guarantees (Atomicity, Consistency, Isolation, Durability) that were previously only available in traditional databases, enforcing constraints that prevent duplicate processing and ensure consistent reads during concurrent writes.~~ — The claim fails on three independent grounds: (1) The historical framing — ACID "previously only available in traditional databases" — is stale; distributed ACID has been established since at least 2010 (Spanner, etc.), predating modern lake formats. (2) The core functional assertion — that modern lakes/warehouses "enforce constraints that prevent duplicate processing" — is directly contradicted by well-documented behavior of Redshift, BigQuery, Snowflake, and Azure Synapse, all of which treat PK/FK constraints as advisory/metadata-only and do not enforce them at the storage layer. Duplicate prevention remains an application/pipeline responsibility. (3) The source is a secondary Airbyte blog post, which is insufficient to support claims of historical novelty or universal constraint enforcement. The quote does logically entail the claim, but the underlying assertions are factually inaccurate, making survival impossible. (<https://airbyte.com/data-engineering-resources/idempotency-in-data-pipelines>)
- ~~Separating persistence from domain model (using two distinct entity classes and mapping layers) increases code volume by 4x or more for CRUD operations and is regretted by teams 1-2 years after implementation.~~ — All three failure conditions are met. (1) Source tier: the URL is a personal blog post — no primary empirical data, no study, no dataset; insufficient for a quantitative claim ("4x code volume") and a behavioral claim ("teams regret it 1-2 years later"). (2) Staleness: the argument pattern originates from 2010–2016 DDD debates; the 2023–2026 landscape has AI-assisted code generation, compile-time mappers, and ORM tooling that structurally invalidate the cost premise. (3) Contradicted: multiple credible counter-sources (Enterprise Craftsmanship, Microsoft .NET microservices docs, 2025 DDD systematic literature review) report net-positive outcomes for complex/long-lived systems and cite no widespread 1-2 year regret pattern. The 4x multiplier is an unsupported rhetorical figure, not a measured result. (<https://victorrentea.ro/blog/overengineering-in-onion-hexagonal-architectures/>)
- ~~In traditional layered architecture, database-driven design forces changes to business logic when persistence implementation changes, creating undesirable coupling.~~ — The quote logically entails the claim, and no sources directly contradict the underlying coupling concern. However, the source is a secondary blog post rather than primary architecture literature (Evans, Cockburn, Fowler), which is insufficient for a prescriptive architectural claim of this weight. Additionally, the claim's framing — that traditional layered/bottom-up design is a distinct, problematic pattern versus top-down domain-first design — reflects a 2003–2012 debate that has softened considerably by 2015–2026, with vertical slice architectures, pragmatic layering, and recognition that coupling severity depends heavily on application complexity. The claim is stale in its binary framing even though the underlying concern (Active Record / DB-driven coupling) remains technically valid. Both source-tier insufficiency and staleness independently satisfy the survives=false criteria per the decision rule. (<https://www.arhohuttunen.com/hexagonal-architecture/>)
